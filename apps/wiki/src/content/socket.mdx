A **socket** is a place on a [[Block]] where a [[Cable]] plugs in. Each socket belongs to one [[Interface]], and the interface decides what the socket looks like.

## Shape and colour

The interface's name is hashed (FNV-1a) into one of eight shapes and one of eight neon colours: 64 combinations. The same interface always looks the same, in every world, on every machine. You can tell two sockets are compatible before reading a label.

The shapes are circle, square, triangle, diamond, hexagon, star, cross and pentagon. When two interfaces in the same world collide, one gets a hand-picked override.

<Question>
64 combinations runs out once a world has more than about 30 interfaces. Options: an engraved glyph, a cable dash pattern, or [[Version Notches]] on the rim.
</Question>

## Male and female

- **Outputs** are male: the shape stands out from the face as a solid plug.
- **Inputs** are female: a recessed ring with the shape cut out of it. An empty one is dark inside. A filled one glows faintly in its colour.

The plug goes into the hole. That's the whole rule, and it reads at a glance.

## Size

Sockets for network [[Cable|cables]] (cable-kind interfaces) are larger than ordinary ones. A thick socket means "this crosses the [[Network Wall]]".

## How many cables

An input declares how many cables it takes:

| Cardinality | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| one | exactly one cable; required | Todo List's `todos` |
| many | any number, in order | App Shell's `main` |
| optional | zero or one | — |

Plugging into a filled *one* socket replaces the old cable rather than failing. Swapping parts is the main thing you do. An output can feed any number of inputs.

## States

A socket pulses brightly while you're holding a [[Wire Coil]] and it's a valid target, and everything else dims. A required input that's empty on the active chain pulses red, and a red beacon rises above its block (see [[Composition]]).
