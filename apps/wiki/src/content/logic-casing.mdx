A **logic casing** is one mounted component. It stands for everything in the function body above the `return`: hooks, derived values, handlers. It's deliberately a black box. You see what goes in and what comes out, not the code.

## Appearance

Dark green glass in a brass frame, with a brass cog inside. Its name floats above it. The cog idles slowly and spins up whenever the component [[Render|renders]].

## Placement

A casing stands on the [[Floors|floor]] for its depth in the tree, centred under its component's box on the [[Screen]]. It's sized to fit that box, with limits so tiny components stay visible and big ones don't become walls.

## Connections

| Where | What | Machine |
|---|---|---|
| bottom | props from the parent | [[Pipe]] |
| side | each `useState` | [[State Tank]] |
| top | props for each child component | [[Pipe]] |
| top | a list of children | [[Telescoping Piston]] |
| top | this component's own pixels | [[Display Link]] |
| alongside the pipes | function props, back down | [[Event Wire]] |

## What counts as "the component's own output"

The JSX a component returns mixes plain elements (`div`, `li`, text) with child components. Child components get their own casings, one floor up. Plain elements don't become machines; they're what the display link paints.
