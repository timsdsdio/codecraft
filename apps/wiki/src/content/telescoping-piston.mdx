When a component renders a list, such as `todos.map((todo) => <TodoItem key={todo.id} … />)`, the list becomes a **telescoping piston**. A rod leaves the [[Logic Casing]] and runs under the list, with one brass head under each item. Each head has an outlet on its side, and a [[Pipe]] from that outlet rises and hooks over into the item's casing on the floor above.

## Behaviour

- **Adding an item** pushes a new head out of the last one, and its side pipe connects.
- **Removing an item** pulls its head back into the next one.
- **Reordering** slides heads along the rod. Heads are keyed, so a moved item keeps its head; this is `key` made visible. With no keys (or index keys), heads would be reused in place and the wrong pipes would light up, which is what happens in React too.
- **Scrolling** slides the whole rod through the casing; see [[Conveyor Belt]] and [[Fold]].

## How a list is recognised

React doesn't keep a record of the `.map` call. The [[Probe]] treats a run of sibling components of the same type that all have keys as one list. That covers the normal way of rendering lists.
